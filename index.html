SS-31
the mitochondrial mechanic

A four-amino-acid peptide that parks itself in your cells' power plants and tries to keep them running clean. In September 2025 it became an FDA-approved drug for the ultra-rare Barth syndrome — after failing trials in heart failure, heart attack, and muscle disease.

Quick read

SS-31 (elamipretide) is a synthetic tetrapeptide that binds cardiolipin, a signature fat in the inner mitochondrial membrane, steadying the cell's energy machinery and cutting oxidative leakage. It earned accelerated FDA approval in 2025 for Barth syndrome, a rare genetic mitochondrial disorder — while larger trials in heart failure, heart attack, and mitochondrial myopathy all missed their main goals. One of the few 'mitochondrial peptides' with genuine human trial data, wins and losses both.

01The peptide

What SS-31 actually is

SS-31 is a tetrapeptide — just four amino acids — engineered to go where almost no drug goes: the inner membrane of your mitochondria, the power plants of the cell. Its clinical name is elamipretide.

Binds cardiolipin in the inner mitochondrial membrane

Four beads, unusual ones

The sequence is D-Arg-Dmt-Lys-Phe-NH₂ — note the D-amino acid and the synthetic Dmt residue. Those odd pieces give it a positive charge and fat-loving character that drag it into mitochondria.

Parks on cardiolipin

Its target is cardiolipin, a fat found almost only in the inner mitochondrial membrane. SS-31 binds it and steadies the membrane where the cell's energy machinery sits.

A drug, not a supplement

Marketed as Forzinity, it won accelerated FDA approval in September 2025 for Barth syndrome. Everything else — longevity clinics, biohacker forums — is off-label or gray-market territory.

Plain English

If the mitochondrion is a power plant, SS-31 is a maintenance engineer who lives on site. It doesn't build new generators — it tightens the bolts on the ones you have so they leak less and hum steadier.

02Discovery

From Cornell chemistry to FDA approval

SS-31 began as a chemistry curiosity about how tiny peptides could slip into mitochondria — then survived twenty years of biotech development, most of it failure, to reach one narrow approval.

Two decades, many failed trials, one ultra-rare approval.
Built for a membrane

The design insight was physical, not biological: give a tiny peptide the right charge and greasiness and mitochondria will pull it in on their own. No transporter needed.

A graveyard of programs

Heart failure, heart attack, primary mitochondrial myopathy — three big programs missed their primary endpoints. Most biotechs don't survive that; this one had Barth syndrome.

The rare-disease path

Barth syndrome affects only a few hundred known patients worldwide. Approval rested on a 12-patient randomized trial plus long-term extension data — tiny by normal standards, huge for an ultra-rare disease.

Plain English

SS-31's résumé reads like a character actor: rejected for every leading role (heart failure, heart attack, muscle disease), then won an award in the smallest possible category — and earned it, for patients with almost no other options.

03Mechanism

How it is supposed to work

The mechanism story is unusually physical for a drug: go to the membrane, hold it steady, leak less energy. Here's the proposed chain.

Proposed chain: target → steady → seal → output. Plausible physics, mixed clinical proof.
The journey: SS-31 crosses into the cell and piles up inside mitochondria — at far higher levels than in the rest of the cell. This delivery story is the least disputed part of the whole picture.

Why the journey matters: most antioxidants float around the whole cell hoping to bump into damage. SS-31 goes to one specific address — the inner mitochondrial membrane — because that is where its docking partner lives.

The docking: SS-31 carries a positive charge; cardiolipin carries a negative one. Opposite charges pull the peptide to exactly the right spot in the inner membrane.

Cardiolipin is the key detail. It is a fat found almost nowhere else in the body, and it acts like an anchor for the energy machinery. When cardiolipin gets damaged, the machinery wobbles. SS-31’s proposed job is to hold the anchor steady.

Inside the powerhouse: SS-31 settles along the folded inner membrane, right where complexes I, III and IV — plus ATP synthase — do their work.

This is also why SS-31 looks nothing like a general antioxidant. Vitamin C or E drift everywhere and mop up damage after it happens. SS-31 parks itself at the exact spot where the damage starts — at least, that is the theory.

The chain in motion: electrons flow down the complexes, protons get pumped across the membrane, and ATP synthase spins that gradient into ATP — the cell’s fuel.

A quick reality check on the animation above: the electron chain really works like this in every cell you own. What is proposed — not proven in humans — is that SS-31 makes this chain run cleaner. The physics is plausible; the clinical proof is narrow.

The core claim, visualized: a steadier membrane leaks fewer free radicals. Animal studies back the “less leak” half — the human half is much thinner.

Oxidative stress is the polite term for cellular rust — and mitochondria are both the furnace and the thing most likely to rust. Less leak sounds great. In animals, it holds up. In people, the evidence narrows to one small approved use.

Same organelle, two states. SS-31’s whole pitch is nudging the left picture toward the right one.

04Trial record

The trials: wins and losses

Most drug pages hide the failures. This one leads with them — because SS-31's failures teach more than its approval does.

Barth syndrome: approved

TAZPOWER randomized just 12 patients; the extension showed sustained gains — about 96 extra meters on the walk test and better heart function. Enough, for an ultra-rare disease, to win accelerated approval.

Three high-profile misses

PROGRESS-HF (71 heart-failure patients): no improvement. EMBRACE-STEMI (118 heart-attack patients): no smaller infarcts. MMPOWER-3 (218 myopathy patients): primary endpoint failed.

The eye program: alive

ReCLAIM-2 in dry macular degeneration missed its primary endpoints — but a retinal secondary signal was strong enough to launch an ongoing phase 3 (RENEW). The most interesting open question.

Plain English

Scorecard: one narrow win, three clear losses, one 'to be continued.' That's not a miracle molecule — it's a real drug with a real, limited job. Anyone selling it as a general energy or longevity fix is selling past the data.

What SS-31 does vs what it doesn’t

What it does (evidence-backed)

  • Concentrates in mitochondria. The delivery physics is the least disputed part. Evidence: strong (lab)
  • Binds cardiolipin in the inner membrane. Evidence: lab + consistent animal data
  • Reduces oxidative damage in animal models of heart, kidney, and muscle disease. Evidence: consistent (animals)
  • Improved walk distance and heart function in Barth syndrome patients. Evidence: narrow human win (n=12)

What it doesn’t do (no human proof)

  • Boost general energy or longevity. Zero human trials support this. Evidence: none
  • Fix heart failure, heart attacks, or myopathy — all three human programs failed. Evidence: negative trials
  • Build new mitochondria. No evidence of increased mitochondrial biogenesis in humans. Evidence: none
  • Improve exercise performance in healthy people. The exercise data is animal-only. Evidence: animals, not humans
Limits of the evidence

The approval rests on 12 patients — accelerated approval means confirmatory data is still owed. Animal wins outnumber human wins by a wide margin. And anything sold outside a pharmacy is unregulated: no quality control, no verified contents.

06Risks & unknowns

Risks & unknowns

Over 2,000 patient-years of trial exposure give SS-31 a better safety file than most peptides here — for the studied populations. Outside them, the file thins fast.

Known

Injection-site reactions

Observed in trials: redness, itching, and irritation where it's injected — the most common adverse events, usually mild and self-limiting.

Known

No mitochondria-specific signal

Observed in trials: across 2,000+ patient-years, no pattern of mitochondrial toxicity (like lactic acidosis) emerged. Reassuring, within studied doses and durations.

Theoretical

Off-label unknowns

Theoretical: trial safety data covers specific diseases, doses, and monitoring. Longevity-clinic and gray-market use has none of that scaffolding — different doses, no monitoring, unknown duration.

Unknown

Pregnancy & young patients

Unknown: insufficient human data in pregnancy and breastfeeding; the approval covers patients 30 kg and up. Blank pages, not clean bills of health.

Unknown

Gray-market quality

Unknown: unregulated 'SS-31' may be underdosed, impure, or mislabeled. For a drug whose real version requires prescription manufacturing, that's a wide quality gap.

Known

Not an energy supplement

Observed fact: the failed trials are data, not bad luck. Treating SS-31 as a general vitality drug means ignoring the studies that said no.

Plain English

Known: itchy injection sites, and a clean-ish trial safety record in studied patients. Unknown: everything about unsupervised, off-label, long-term use. The drug earned trust in a hospital; the forums are spending it without a license.

07Versus

SS-31 vs the field

Five contenders, one question each: where does it act, does it target mitochondria, and what is the evidence? No hype — just the biological differences.

SS-31 vs MOTS-c

peptide vs peptide
Where it acts
SS-31 parks in the inner mitochondrial membrane. MOTS-c — a peptide encoded by mitochondrial DNA — travels in the blood and signals to metabolic pathways throughout the body.
Targets mitochondria directly?
SS-31: yes — it concentrates there and binds cardiolipin. MOTS-c: not really — it is made by mitochondria but works as a long-distance messenger, mostly through AMPK-related metabolic signaling.
Evidence quality
SS-31: one narrow human approval (Barth, n=12) plus failed trials. MOTS-c: animal studies plus small early human work on metabolism — no approvals, no large trials.
The key difference
Messenger vs mechanic. MOTS-c sends metabolic signals; SS-31 physically shores up the membrane. Different jobs entirely.

SS-31 vs CoQ10

targeted peptide vs electron carrier
Where it acts
CoQ10 (ubiquinone) sits inside the electron transport chain itself, shuttling electrons between complexes — and doubles as a general antioxidant everywhere else.
Targets mitochondria directly?
Sort of. CoQ10 works inside mitochondria but also everywhere else; it does not concentrate in mitochondria the way SS-31 does. (Specialized versions add targeting; plain CoQ10 does not.)
Evidence quality
Decades of mixed human data — some support in heart failure, plenty of nulls elsewhere. Far more human data than SS-31, but messier.
The key difference
CoQ10 is a part that wears out and gets replaced; SS-31 is a stabilizer for the structure around the parts.

SS-31 vs NAC

targeted peptide vs antioxidant precursor
Where it acts
NAC works systemically as a building block for glutathione — the body’s own master antioxidant.
Targets mitochondria directly?
No. NAC raises antioxidant supply everywhere, with no mitochondrial targeting at all.
Evidence quality
Established medicine for acetaminophen overdose; mixed-to-weak everywhere else it has been tried.
The key difference
NAC refills the body’s general antioxidant tank; SS-31 tries to stop the leak at the source instead of mopping up afterward.

SS-31 vs glutathione

targeted peptide vs master antioxidant
Where it acts
Glutathione works throughout the cell and body, neutralizing free radicals directly. Supplemental forms are poorly absorbed when swallowed.
Targets mitochondria directly?
No. It goes everywhere — no special mitochondrial address.
Evidence quality
Rock-solid biology (your cells genuinely depend on it), weak supplementation data — raising blood levels barely moves the needle inside cells.
The key difference
Glutathione mops up damage after it happens; SS-31 aims to prevent the damage from starting. Cleanup crew vs structural repair.

SS-31 vs PQQ & general antioxidants

targeted peptide vs redox support
Where it acts
PQQ, vitamin C, vitamin E and friends circulate systemically as general redox support.
Targets mitochondria directly?
No — none of them carry a mitochondrial address label.
Evidence quality
Thin: mostly cell and animal work, small inconsistent human studies.
The key difference
Generic fire extinguishers vs a sprinkler installed in one specific room. General antioxidants treat the whole house; SS-31 only knows one room.
The honest summary: none of these do SS-31’s specific job — nothing else binds cardiolipin to steady the inner membrane. And SS-31 doesn’t do their jobs either. They are different tools with different evidence and different open questions. Anyone framing one as the “better SS-31” is selling a story, not a comparison.
08Potential benefits

Potential benefits, tier by tier

What could SS-31 actually do for someone? The honest answer depends on which universe of evidence you're standing in. Three tiers, kept strictly separate — because presenting them as equals would be misleading.

Think of it this way: evidence tiers are like three different courtrooms — one where the verdict is already in, one where the trial is still running, and one where people are just swapping stories in the hallway.

Tier 1 · Established — real human data

Established · human data

Barth syndrome — the one approved indication

  • Better walking distance. In the TAZPOWER trial and its long extension, patients gained about 96 extra meters on the 6-minute walk test — sustained, not a blip. Evidence: human trial + extension (n=12)
  • Better heart function. The same program showed improved cardiac measures — the organ Barth syndrome hits hardest. Evidence: same narrow trial program
  • A real approval behind it. The FDA granted accelerated approval in September 2025 on these data. Regulators do not move on wishes. Evidence: regulatory fact

This tier covers exactly one disease: Barth syndrome. Everything else on this page lives in the tiers below — and that distinction matters.

Think of it this way: accelerated approval is like a provisional license — granted after a short but real test, with the full exam (confirmatory evidence) still owed.

Tier 2 · Predicted / emerging — honest about the gaps

Predicted · emerging

Where the science points, cautiously

  • Vision (dry macular degeneration). ReCLAIM-2 missed its primary endpoints, but a retinal secondary signal was strong enough to launch the ongoing phase 3 RENEW trial. Promising — and still unproven. Status: being tested now
  • Exercise and aging. Aged and heart-failure animal models repeatedly show better exercise capacity and muscle energetics. This is exactly the kind of result that most often fails to cross into humans. Status: animals only — a prediction, not data
  • Heart failure, heart attack, myopathy. Three human trials said no. Any benefit claimed here is speculation against negative data — keep it labeled as such. Status: negative trials

The mechanism is plausible and the animal physics is elegant — but elegant physics has failed humans before, including in this very program.

Think of it this way: an animal result is a sketch, not a photograph of what happens in people. It shows the idea can work — not that it will.

09Who it may suit

Who it may suit — and who it should not

Based on the research record — not on forum enthusiasm — here is where SS-31 has a real footing, and where it does not.

May be relevant for (approved / research contexts)

  • People with Barth syndrome. The one approved indication (accelerated FDA approval, September 2025) — care that belongs with specialist clinicians.
  • Clinical-trial participants. People with dry macular degeneration in the phase 3 RENEW trial, or in other controlled research settings — under medical supervision.
  • Researchers studying mitochondrial disease. SS-31 remains a useful research tool for probing cardiolipin and inner-membrane biology.
  • Readers learning the science. Understanding the evidence tiers above is the right starting point before forming any opinion.

May not suit / extra caution (no established basis)

  • Healthy people chasing general energy or longevity. Zero human trials support this — the big programs failed.
  • Anyone expecting a performance or recovery boost. The exercise data is animal-only; the human muscle-disease trial missed.
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding people. Insufficient data — a blank page, not a clean bill of health.
  • Anyone considering gray-market product. Unknown contents and purity, no monitoring, none of the trial safety scaffolding.
  • People with complex conditions on other treatments. Effects outside the studied populations are uncharted territory.

Think of it this way: SS-31 is a specialist's instrument tuned for one rare disease, not a Swiss army knife for wellness. A scalpel is a superb tool — in the wrong hands, in the wrong room, it isn't.

This is general information, not personal medical advice. The approved indication — Barth syndrome, September 2025 — is a medical decision for specialist clinicians. Everything else here is educational context, not a recommendation for or against anything.
